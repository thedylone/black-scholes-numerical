02 — Model

A call option as a diffusion problem

A European call gives the right to buy a stock at the strike K at the expiry T. If the stock follows geometric Brownian motion with volatility \sigma and the risk-free rate is r, its fair value V(S,t) satisfies the Black–Scholes equation

\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2S^2\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} + rS\frac{\partial V}{\partial S} - rV = 0.

It is a convection–diffusion equation that runs backwards: we know the payoff at expiry and want the value today. Substituting the time to expiry \tau = T - t (so \partial_t = -\partial_\tau) turns it into a forward problem,

-V_\tau + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2S^2V_{SS} + rSV_S - rV = 0,

with the payoff as the initial condition and two conditions in price:

V(S,0)=\max\{S-K,\,0\},\qquad V(0,\tau)=0,\qquad V(S_{\max},\tau)= S_{\max}-Ke^{-r\tau}.

The last condition is the large-S asymptote applied at a finite S_{\max}. That truncation is why the error plot rises towards the right-hand edge, especially for long maturities or high volatility (try the notebook preset).

03 — Grid

The grid

Prices are sampled at S_j = jh with h = S_{\max}/N, and time to expiry at \tau_i = ik with k = T/M. As in the notebook, two coefficients collect the repeated factors:

\alpha = \frac{\sigma^2}{2h^2}, \qquad \beta = \frac{r}{h}.

Both schemes use a forward difference in \tau and central differences in S.

04 — Explicit

Explicit finite differences

Evaluating every S-derivative at the known level \tau gives

-\frac{V_{S,\tau+k}-V_{S,\tau}}{k} + \alpha S^2\left(V_{S+h,\tau}-2V_{S,\tau}+V_{S-h,\tau}\right) + \beta S\,\frac{V_{S+h,\tau}-V_{S-h,\tau}}{2} - rV_{S,\tau} = 0,

so each new value is a weighted sum of three old ones:

V_{S,\tau+k} = k\Big[\big(\alpha S^2-\tfrac{\beta S}{2}\big)V_{S-h,\tau} + \big(\tfrac{1}{k}-2\alpha S^2-r\big)V_{S,\tau} + \big(\alpha S^2+\tfrac{\beta S}{2}\big)V_{S+h,\tau}\Big].

Why it blows up

The weight on V_{S,\tau} must stay positive at every node. Otherwise errors change sign from one step to the next and grow geometrically. The worst node is S = S_{\max} = Nh, which gives

\frac{1}{k} - \frac{\sigma^2 S_{\max}^2}{h^2} - r > 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \lambda = k\left(\sigma^2N^2 + r\right) < 1.

This CFL-type limit is quadratic in the grid resolution: doubling N forces four times as many time steps. The notebook's fine grid (h = 0.01, N = 500, \sigma = 0.8) would need k < 6.25\times10^{-6}, or 1.6 million steps over ten years. With k = 0.01 it gives \lambda \approx 1600. Load the Notebook fine grid preset and scrub the time slider to watch the oscillation start at the right-hand edge and swamp the solution.

05 — Crank–Nicolson

Crank–Nicolson

Crank–Nicolson averages each derivative, and the rV term, between the two time levels:

V_S \approx \tfrac{1}{4h}\left(V_{S+h,\tau+k}-V_{S-h,\tau+k}+V_{S+h,\tau}-V_{S-h,\tau}\right),\quad V_{SS} \approx \tfrac{1}{2h^2}\left(\delta^2 V_{\tau+k} + \delta^2 V_{\tau}\right),\quad V \approx \tfrac12\left(V_{S,\tau+k}+V_{S,\tau}\right).

Here \delta^2 V = V_{S+h}-2V_{S}+V_{S-h}. Collecting the unknowns on the left leaves one tridiagonal system per time step:

\big(\tfrac{\alpha S^2}{2}-\tfrac{\beta S}{4}\big)V_{S-h,\tau+k} + \big(-\tfrac1k-\alpha S^2-\tfrac r2\big)V_{S,\tau+k} + \big(\tfrac{\alpha S^2}{2}+\tfrac{\beta S}{4}\big)V_{S+h,\tau+k} = B_S(V_\tau).

The scheme is unconditionally stable and second-order accurate in both h and k. The notebook builds the full matrix and calls np.linalg.solve, which costs O(N^3) per step. This port uses the Thomas algorithm, which costs O(N), so even a 500 × 20 000 grid solves in a fraction of a second.
